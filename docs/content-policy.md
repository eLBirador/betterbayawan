# Content accuracy and sources

Review date: October 21, 2026. BetterBayawan is an independent community project,
not an official government transaction channel.

## Evidence before publication

- Preserve the brand names BetterBayawan, BetterBayawan.org, and Better Bayawan
  unchanged in every language, including logo descriptions and sentences.
- Mark brand-only markup with `translate="no" data-no-translate`, including
  styled names split across nested elements.
- Use the original law for a legal date, PSA for census/PSGC data, the named city
  financial statement for amounts, and the city directory for office holders.
- Record the URL, source edition/period, and date checked. A generic agency
  homepage is not enough evidence for a specific fee or numerical claim.
- Describe published Charter timings as dated standards. Distinguish staff
  handling time, mandatory posting, external processing, and actual release.
- Do not present an unsourced estimate as a fee, eligibility rule, guaranteed
  benefit, emergency response time, or government commitment.
- When sources disagree, explain the conflict and link to the responsible office.
  Never silently amend a quoted legislative title.
- Historical datasets and selected legislative records must be labeled as such.
  Archived events and vacancies must not look like currently open opportunities.

## Open verification limits

The 2025 Charter is a published reference, not proof that no subsequent fee or
office issuance exists. The 2021 poverty figure remains explicitly historical;
the newer PSA release is linked without guessing a replacement Bayawan value.
The 2025 tax-amnesty catalogue conflicts with a council agenda about its end
date; the period is past and no current amnesty is promised. Local implementation
of the September 2026 unified PWD-ID rollout must be confirmed with CSWDO/PDAO.
Unverified CMCI scores and template rental prices were removed from publication.

Every later correction should update the relevant data record, both translations,
and the audit checklist when applicable. Tests can check consistency and source
presence; they cannot certify that a telephone number is currently answered or
that an agency has not issued a newer rule.
