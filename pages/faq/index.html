Frequently Asked Questions

General Questions

What are the office hours of the City Hall?

Regular city-office hours are Monday to Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, excluding holidays. Frontline transactions are subject to the No Noon Break policy. Ask the responsible office about any transaction-specific schedule.

How can I contact a specific city office?

Visit our Government Directory page to find contact information for all city offices and department heads.

Can I request services online?

The city links to Filipizen for supported business and tax transactions. Use the official portal and check the responsible office’s payment and documentary instructions. Other services may require an in-person visit.

Certificates & Documents

How long does it take to get a birth certificate?

The published 2025 Bayawan Citizen’s Charter lists four hours for local certified civil-registry copies. This is different from a PSA copy requested through BREQS or an online delivery provider. See the Civil Registrar guide for the source and published fees.

Can someone else request my certificate for me?

The 2025 Charter provides for authorized representatives. Bring the original authorization letter and the required identification and photocopies for the concerned party and representative. Ask the Civil Registrar about accepted alternative identification.

What is the difference between PSA and local civil registrar certificates?

Both are certified true copies. PSA certificates are the nationally-recognized version required for passport and visa applications. Local civil registrar certificates are accepted for most local transactions and are often processed faster.

Business & Permits

When should I renew my business permit?

January 20 is the ordinary renewal reference, but extensions are year-specific. A published 2026 council resolution extended processing through January 31, 2026. That period is past; check BPLO’s current-year advisory before relying on any deadline.

What do I need to start a new business in Bayawan City?

Use the new-business checklist in the 2025 City Citizen’s Charter, printed page 304. Requirements depend on business type, premises, and sector. The detailed BPLO guide links the original checklist and distinguishes integrated clearances from documents the applicant supplies.

How can I pay my real property tax?

The city links to Filipizen for supported business and tax transactions. Use the official portal and check the responsible office’s payment and documentary instructions. Other services may require an in-person visit.

Social Services

How do I apply for a Senior Citizen ID?

The 2025 local Charter lists two 1×1 photographs and valid ID, birth certificate, or voter’s certification. It lists no fee, 30 minutes for forms, and seven working days for the required signatures. Ask CSWDO/OSCA about current arrangements.

What benefits do senior citizens receive?

The 20% discount and applicable VAT exemption apply to covered goods and services for the senior citizen’s exclusive use. There is no general minimum-purchase condition here. Other benefits have their own rules; consult NCSC or OSCA for the applicable category.

About the Developer

Who developed Better Bayawan?

Rodney Dela Cruz is a local student and full-stack developer from Bayawan City, Negros Oriental. He built BetterBayawan.org as a community-driven initiative to build a digital bridge between the residents of Bayawan City and the local government. Rodney specializes in full-stack development and automation, and has worked on open-source civic-tech projects that help modernize Philippine local governments.

BetterBayawan.org is part of the BetterGov.ph BetterLGU initiative and follows a template based on the open-source BetterSolano.org by Ramon Logan, which was modified and adapted for Bayawan City. The project carries a version badge in the site footer, and the source is maintained openly so improvements are transparent and reproducible.

BetterBayawan.org is an open-source project released under MIT | CC BY 4.0 to empower community-driven development. Contributions are warmly welcomed from everyone — whether you are a developer, data researcher, designer, content writer, translator, or a concerned citizen of Bayawan City — your participation helps shape the project for all.

Please note that BetterBayawan.org is an independent, open-source community project. It is not affiliated with, funded by, or endorsed by the Bayawan City government. All content is based on publicly available information sourced from official government portals. For official transactions and authoritative updates, always refer directly to your local government office.

For Developers

Is the source code open-source?

Yes. BetterBayawan.org is a fully open-source, community-driven project. The complete source code is publicly available so that anyone can inspect how the site works, verify the information it publishes, and reuse the templates for their own community. As an open civic-tech project, it is built to be shared and improved upon by others — not locked away.

What license is Better Bayawan released under?

BetterBayawan.org is released under a dual license: the MIT License for the software (code, scripts, and technical infrastructure) and Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) for the content (text, data, and documentation). This dual approach means the code can be freely used, modified, and redistributed, while the published content can be reused provided proper attribution is given to the source.

What technology stack is used?

The site uses static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. One build combines shared layouts, source-linked JSON data, and English, Filipino, and Cebuano dictionaries.
